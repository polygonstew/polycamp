A grey cabin and a coloured cabin are the same geometry. Materials are why one looks like a school project
and the other looks like a place. They are also where Blender gets its reputation for "nodes", the boxes and
wires everyone finds scary for about forty minutes.

This world covers the **Principled BSDF** (the one shader that does most things), the **Shader Editor** where
you wire nodes together, **procedural** materials that need no image files at all (so they are cheap, resolution
independent and perfect for a machine without much memory), **emission and glass** for the lantern, and the
**world** that lights and colours the sky.

At the end the bare grey diorama from the last world gets proper wood, stone, grass, shingles and a lantern that actually glows.
